# Remote and Hybrid Working Policy

## Executive summary

Northline is hybrid by default for employees assigned to Manchester, Amsterdam and Austin hubs. Hub-based employees are normally expected in their assigned office **2 days per week**, with Tuesday and Thursday as common collaboration days unless the team has an approved alternative. Local core collaboration hours are **10:00–15:00** on working days.

Northline provides standard IT equipment and a home-office allowance of **£600 / $750 / €700 once every 36 months** for approved ergonomic or workspace equipment. The allowance requires receipts and manager approval and follows `NL-FIN-EXPENSE-2026`.

## Scope

This policy applies to employees whose contract permits hybrid or remote work. It does not change the employee's employing entity, contractual work location, immigration status or tax residence.

## Work patterns

### Hub-based hybrid employees

A hub-based employee should normally attend the assigned office two days per week. Tuesday and Thursday are default collaboration days. A function may substitute other anchor days where customer coverage, engineering operations or time-zone needs make a different pattern more practical.

Managers should focus on predictable collaboration rather than badge-counting. Occasional weeks with less office attendance are acceptable because of travel, illness, annual leave, family needs or focused project work.

### Fully remote employees

A role may be designated fully remote where approved by the functional director and People Operations. The employee's contract or written remote-work letter identifies the approved country and primary work location.

Working permanently from another country or US state without approval is not permitted merely because the role is remote.

## Core hours and availability

Local core collaboration hours are **10:00–15:00** in the employee's local office time zone, Monday to Friday on working days. Employees may arrange the rest of their working day around customer coverage, meetings and role requirements.

Core hours are not an instruction to work beyond contracted hours. Teams spanning regions should rotate inconvenient meeting times rather than making one region permanently absorb early or late calls.

Customer Support follows its separate queue hours in `NL-CS-SUPPORT-PLAYBOOK-2026`.

## Workspace and safety

Employees are responsible for maintaining a reasonably safe, private and reliable home workspace. Confidential calls should not be conducted where unauthorised people can overhear sensitive customer or employee information.

Company laptops must be used in accordance with `NL-SEC-AUP-2026`. Public Wi-Fi should be avoided for sensitive work unless the approved security controls are in place.

## Equipment

Northline normally supplies a managed laptop, power adapter and security key where required. Depending on role, IT may also supply a monitor, dock, headset or keyboard.

The home-office allowance is **£600 in the UK, $750 in the US and €700 in the Netherlands/EU once every 36 months**. The allowance may be used for an approved desk, chair, monitor arm, lighting, keyboard, mouse or similar work equipment.

The allowance may not be used for televisions, gaming equipment, home decoration, household furniture unrelated to work or duplicate company equipment without approval.

Items purchased directly by Northline remain company property. Items reimbursed through the allowance are treated according to local company practice and the written approval.

## Internet and utilities

Employees are expected to maintain ordinary home internet suitable for their role. Northline does not reimburse standard household electricity, rent or broadband by default.

A function may approve a business-grade connection where the role requires it, such as a dedicated connection for a support or demo environment.

## Temporary work from another location

An employee may work temporarily from another location in the same approved country with manager agreement, provided security and working-hour requirements can still be met.

Work from another country requires advance People Operations approval even for a short period. As a company guideline, cross-border remote work is limited to **20 working days per calendar year** unless People Operations approves a different arrangement. Approval may be refused because of immigration, employment, payroll, data-transfer, insurance or customer restrictions.

The 20-day guideline is not an entitlement and does not override local law.

## Travel to offices

Ordinary travel between home and the employee's contractual office is commuting and is not reimbursable under the expense policy. Travel to another Northline office, a customer site or a temporary work location may be business travel depending on the circumstances and prior approval.

## Performance and communication

Remote and hybrid employees are held to the same role expectations as office-based employees. Managers should document outcomes, response expectations and meeting norms rather than assuming physical presence equals performance.

Employees should keep calendars current, use status indicators reasonably and tell the team when they will be unavailable during core hours.

## Confidentiality at home

Paper containing Confidential information should be minimised. If printing is genuinely required, the employee must store and dispose of it securely.

Family members, housemates and visitors must not use company devices. Screens should be positioned to reduce casual viewing of customer or employee data.

## Accessibility and adjustments

Employees who need a workplace adjustment should contact People Operations. Approved equipment or attendance adjustments related to disability or another protected need are handled separately from the standard home-office allowance and are not constrained by the £600 / $750 / €700 cap where a greater adjustment is appropriate.

## Meetings and hybrid etiquette

Teams should make meetings workable for remote participants. A room full of office attendees should still use the shared meeting link and appropriate room audio so that remote colleagues can hear and contribute. Decisions made after a meeting should be recorded in the normal team system rather than left only in corridor conversation.

Managers may designate specific in-person planning, customer or team days with reasonable notice. Where an employee has an approved remote-work or accessibility arrangement, the manager should check with People Operations before treating non-attendance as a performance issue.

## Weather, transport disruption and office closure

If severe weather, public transport disruption, building access problems or another local event makes travel unsafe or impractical, hub-based employees should normally work remotely where they can do so safely. The office lead may designate a temporary remote day without changing the employee's normal hybrid status.

An employee who cannot work because both the office and their home setup are unavailable should contact their manager. The manager and People Operations will decide whether the time is working time, emergency leave, annual leave or another category based on the circumstances.

## New starters

Managers should plan onboarding so a new starter has a managed device, identity access and named contacts before meaningful remote work begins. The first two weeks may include more office attendance than the normal two-day pattern where useful for onboarding, but this should be communicated before the start date.

No new starter should be asked to use a personal computer to access production while waiting for a company laptop.

## Exceptions and review

Functional directors and People Operations may approve exceptions to attendance or location rules. Security approval is also required where the exception changes the device or data-access model.

Policy owner: Head of People. Operational partners: IT, Security and Facilities.
