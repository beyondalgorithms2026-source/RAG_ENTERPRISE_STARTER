# Code of Conduct and Speak-Up Policy

## Executive summary

Northline expects people to act honestly, respectfully and in the company's and customers' legitimate interests. Harassment, discrimination, retaliation, fraud, bribery, deliberate misuse of data and conflicts that are hidden from the company are not acceptable.

Employees can raise concerns with their manager, People Operations, Security, Finance leadership or the confidential speak-up mailbox. Retaliation against a person who raises a concern in good faith or participates in an investigation is prohibited.

## Scope

The code applies to employees, directors, contractors and temporary staff when acting for Northline, using Northline systems, attending work events or interacting with colleagues, customers, suppliers or partners.

## Respectful workplace

Treat colleagues and external contacts with dignity. Northline does not tolerate harassment, bullying, discrimination, threats or unwanted sexual conduct.

Workplace decisions should be based on legitimate role and business factors rather than protected personal characteristics. People Operations should be involved where a manager is unsure how to handle an allegation or accommodation request.

## Honest records and communications

Business records must be accurate enough for their purpose. Do not falsify timesheets, expenses, invoices, CRM stages, security evidence, support timestamps or financial approvals.

Do not knowingly make misleading statements to a customer about product capability, incident status, roadmap commitment, security certification or contractual entitlement.

## Conflicts of interest

Disclose a situation where personal, family or financial interests could affect—or reasonably appear to affect—a Northline decision. Examples include selecting a supplier owned by a close relative, managing a family member, or holding a material financial interest in a vendor being evaluated.

A disclosed conflict is not automatically misconduct. Hiding it is the larger problem. The manager and People Operations or Finance will decide whether recusal or another control is needed.

## Gifts and hospitality

Modest, infrequent business hospitality may be acceptable. Cash and cash-equivalent gifts are not acceptable.

An employee should obtain manager approval before giving or accepting a gift or hospitality item worth more than **£100 / $125 / €115 per person**. Anything above **£250 / $315 / €290** also requires Finance approval.

Gifts or hospitality must never be used to influence an improper decision. Stricter customer or public-sector rules take priority.

## Company assets and data

Use company assets responsibly. Follow `NL-SEC-AUP-2026` for devices, access, data classification and AI tools. Customer and employee data may be accessed only for a legitimate work purpose.

## Competition and commercial behaviour

Do not agree with competitors to fix prices, divide customers, coordinate bids or exchange competitively sensitive non-public information. Escalate unusual competitor conversations to Commercial Operations.

## Speak-up channels

A concern may be raised through any of these channels:

- line manager;
- another manager;
- People Operations;
- Head of Security for security or data concerns;
- Finance leadership for fraud, bribery, financial control or vendor concerns;
- confidential speak-up mailbox: `speakup@northline.example`.

The mailbox is a synthetic address for this corpus.

A report does not need to use legal terminology. The reporter should share what happened, when, who was involved and any evidence they already have. They should not conduct covert access or break security controls to gather more evidence.

## Anonymous reports

Northline will accept anonymous reports where the channel permits them, but anonymity can limit the ability to ask follow-up questions. The company will still review the available information.

## Non-retaliation

Retaliation is prohibited against someone who raises a concern in good faith, asks for advice, refuses to participate in suspected misconduct or supports an investigation. Retaliation includes dismissal, demotion, threats, exclusion, adverse scheduling or other material disadvantage because of the report.

A report made in good faith is protected even if the concern is not substantiated. Deliberately false allegations may themselves be misconduct.

## Investigations

Northline will assign an investigator with appropriate independence. Information will be shared only with people who need it for the investigation, decision, legal obligations or safety.

Employees are expected to cooperate honestly and preserve relevant records. No one should delete messages or documents after becoming aware of an investigation or hold request.

The company may take interim measures to protect people, systems or evidence. An interim measure is not automatically a disciplinary finding.

## Outcomes

Where misconduct is substantiated, outcomes may include coaching, control changes, repayment, access restriction, warning, reassignment, termination of employment or contract, customer/vendor remediation, or referral to external authorities where appropriate.

## Manager duties

A manager who receives a serious concern should listen, avoid promising absolute confidentiality, record the key facts and route the matter promptly. The manager must not conduct an amateur investigation into allegations involving harassment, fraud, retaliation or serious security misconduct without the appropriate function.

## Outside work and personal activity

Employees may have outside interests, volunteering or secondary work where it does not materially interfere with Northline duties, misuse company information or create an unmanaged conflict. Work for a direct competitor, paid advisory work involving a Northline customer or vendor, or an outside role likely to use Northline Confidential information must be disclosed to People Operations before starting.

Personal political, charitable or community activity must not be presented as a Northline position unless the company has authorised it. Company funds, customer lists and work systems should not be used for personal campaigning.

## Social media and public statements

Employees may discuss their own work experience in ordinary personal terms, but must not disclose Confidential information or claim to speak for Northline without authority. Media enquiries and requests for formal company comment should be routed to the designated communications owner.

Screenshots of internal tools, customer names, incident channels, unreleased product screens and non-public financial figures are not appropriate personal social-media content.

## Suppliers and procurement integrity

Employees involved in selecting a vendor must follow `NL-PROC-VENDOR-2026` and disclose personal relationships or benefits offered by bidders. A vendor should not be selected because it gave the evaluator gifts, personal discounts or entertainment.

Bank-detail changes and unusual payment instructions should be treated as control issues, not inconveniences to bypass. Finance may pause payment while it verifies the request.

## Security and responsible reporting

Employees who discover a security weakness in the course of normal work should report it to Security. They should not expand the test beyond what is necessary to describe the issue unless authorised. Accessing another employee's or customer's data “to prove the point” can itself be misconduct.

A person who accidentally receives information they should not have should stop using it and report the access problem rather than forwarding the data to colleagues.

## Leadership responsibilities

Managers and executives are expected to model the same controls they ask others to follow. Seniority does not create an exception to expense approval, data handling, respectful conduct or investigation rules.

A manager who learns of possible retaliation, harassment, fraud, bribery, serious safety risk or deliberate security misuse must route the concern promptly even if the reporter asks the manager to “keep it off the record”. The manager can explain that information will be limited to people who need it, but cannot promise that a serious issue will never be acted on.

## Questions and exceptions

There is no exception allowing bribery, retaliation, falsification or deliberate security misuse. Questions about legitimate gifts, conflicts or outside activities should be raised before acting where practical.

## Ownership

Owner: Head of People. Finance, Security and Commercial Operations support investigations within their subject areas. This policy supersedes `NL-HR-CODE-2025`.
